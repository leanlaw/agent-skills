# Draft checks

Light checks for entries likely to be questioned at pre-bill review or by the client. They
are flags for the attorney to look at, not rules. A flagged entry can be perfectly fine.

## Possible block billing

An entry of **more than 3.0 hours** whose narrative lists several distinct tasks (joined
by `;`, `,`, "and", or "/") with no time split between them. Many clients' billing
guidelines reject these, and reviewers often cut them.

A long entry for **one** task ("Attend deposition of J. Ruiz") is not block billing. Don't
flag it.

## Vague narrative

Flag when the narrative doesn't let a client tell what was done or why. Signs:

- Fewer than four words, or only a verb: "Research", "Review", "Emails", "Call".
- Generic phrases with no object: "work on matter", "attention to file", "various
  tasks", "follow up", "prepare documents", "review file".
- No person, document or issue named where one is expected, e.g. "call with client" vs
  "call with J. Ruiz re: settlement terms".
- Placeholder text: "test", "tbd", "x", a single punctuation mark, or empty.

## Not flags

- Short narratives on **non-billable** entries.
- LEDES task/activity codes: don't judge the codes themselves.
- Rates and amounts: the reviewer sees them on the pre-bill.

## How to present a flag

One line per entry: date, matter, hours, the narrative in quotes, and the reason in a
few words. Don't propose a rewritten narrative. The attorney knows what they did and the
skill doesn't.
