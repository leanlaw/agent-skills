# Activity sources

How to read each source, what counts as work, and how long to estimate it took. Estimates
are starting points for the attorney to correct, not measurements. When a rule here and
the attorney's own history disagree, follow the history: if they always log 0.2 for a
client email, a client email is 0.2.

## Calendar

Read every event in the period from the attorney's primary calendar, and any other calendar
they say they use for work.

**Counts as work:**

- Events the attorney accepted, organized or attended, with at least one other person, or
  with a title that names a client, matter or task.
- Solo blocks with a work title, such as "Draft MSJ - Ruiz". Calendar blocks are plans,
  not proof, so give these **Medium** confidence at best and flag them "planned time".

**Leave out:**

- Declined events, and events the attorney was optional on and didn't respond to.
- All-day events, out-of-office, working location, and focus or "Hold" blocks without a work
  title.
- Personal events: medical, family, school, gym, meals without a client, travel booked as
  personal. Count them; don't name them.
- Events marked private, whatever the title says.
- Recurring firm events the attorney's history never logs, such as a weekly all-hands they
  don't record. Note the first one in the "not proposed" list so they can say otherwise.

**Duration:** the scheduled length. If the attorney's history shows they log a recurring
meeting shorter than scheduled, use that instead. Add travel only if a separate travel
event exists.

**People:** keep every attendee's email address and the organizer's, including on invites
the attorney received from someone outside the firm. These addresses are what Step 4 looks
up against client contacts, and they place a meeting more reliably than its title. Read
the invite description too, for a matter name, reference or dial-in title. Ignore the
attorney's own address and other addresses at the firm's domain, except to tell internal
meetings from client meetings: a meeting where everyone is at the firm is internal unless
the title names a client or matter.

## Sent email

Search the attorney's **sent** mail for the period. Received mail isn't work until they
answer it, and the answer is in sent mail.

**Group into threads.** Every message the attorney sent in one thread on one day is one
block. A thread that runs over several days is one block per day.

**Leave out:**

- Calendar responses, automatic replies, and one-word acknowledgements ("Thanks!", "Got
  it") on their own. A thread with only these isn't work.
- Messages to the firm's own domain that are purely administrative (scheduling, IT,
  expenses), unless they name a client or matter.
- Personal mail.

**Duration:** start at **0.1 per substantive message**, and more when the message shows
more work:

| Shows | Estimate |
|---|---|
| A short reply or a forward with a line of instruction | 0.1 |
| A reply of a few paragraphs, or one answering a legal question | 0.2–0.3 |
| An attachment the attorney evidently drafted or revised (a redline, "attached is the draft") | 0.5, and note it in the narrative |

Cap a single thread-day at 1.0 unless the history shows longer for that kind of work.
Email time is the most often under-recorded and the easiest to over-claim, so stay
conservative and let the attorney raise it.

**People:** the To and Cc addresses, for client matching in Step 4.

## Slack and Teams

Use only if connected, and only messages the attorney **sent**.

- Group the attorney's messages by conversation and day, like email threads.
- Most chat is internal coordination. Propose a block only when the conversation names a
  client or matter, or is in a channel named for one, or is with a client in a shared
  channel.
- Estimate 0.1 per conversation-day, or 0.2 for a long substantive exchange. Chat rarely
  justifies more without other evidence.

## Combining sources

After building blocks from every source, merge the ones that describe the same work:

- An email sent within an hour after a meeting, to the same people, about the same
  subject, is follow-up. Keep it as its own block on the same matter, with its own
  estimate. Don't fold it into the meeting time.
- The same work showing in two sources, such as a Teams call that is also a calendar event,
  is one block. Keep the calendar duration.
- Blocks on one matter on one day can be combined into one entry if that's how the
  attorney's history logs that matter. Otherwise keep them separate: separate entries are
  easier for the attorney to check and easier for the client to read.

Never let one day's proposals plus its logged time run past the hours the activity spans.
If a day's calendar ran 9 to 5 and proposals would take it to 11 hours, the estimates are
too high. Cut the email estimates first.
